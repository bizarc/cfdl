02
The approach

Two of your own implementations agreeing is not evidence

Every benchmark used to compare CFDL against a second script we wrote ourselves. When both sides share an assumption, they agree forever and the assumption is never tested. That is exactly what happened — three separate defects survived for months because our own reference restated the same misreading. So we changed what we check against.

1
Obtain and reconcile privately. Read the source, or run the external engine in a scratch directory outside the repo.
2
Build a CFDL model and reconcile it line by line — recording what diverged, because that is where the defects are.
3
Commit only the evidence: the model, anchor figures, and a note recording the reconciliation.
4
The mechanism never enters the repo. No vendored tool, no CI dependency, no copy of a licensed document.

Three rules we hold to

Every asserted number comes from outside. Cases reconciled externally carry no reference script at all — writing one would turn an independent check back into a self-check.

We never loosen a tolerance to pass. Where we differ, we find out why and write it down. Two divergences so far were the source's, not ours.

We don't guess at conventions. A formula we cannot derive is left unasserted and recorded as open, rather than fitted until it matches.

Why this is worth the effort: of the eleven defects found this cycle, not one could have been caught by our own reference scripts. Several had been wrong since the first release.

Accuracy

How close is close?

In every case the remaining gap is the source's precision, not ours. Where a source publishes to the cent we match to the cent; where it rounds, we sit on the rounding floor.

CaseResult Worst gapWhat explains it
MIT PS1 Every pro forma line; present value $2,292,810.18 vs published $2,292,810 $0.18 The source is printed to whole dollars. We are exact.
MBS conventions Interest, amortisation, prepayment and recovery across a 30-year pool; 336 of 336 recoveries agree $0.51 Published table is rounded to whole dollars, so half a dollar is the tightest bound it can support.
Auto ABS All 8 published pay-down points; weighted average life 0.3695 vs 0.37 yrs; pool balance exact to the cent 0.004 pts Source rounds to 0.01 percentage points — 0.005 is the floor a reader could check.

NREL SAM Two cases: PPA and merchant revenue, O&M, project debt, depreciation, capacity payments, and the tax credit's statutory staircase $0.0000009 Our decimal arithmetic against their floating point. Depreciation matched exactly, every period.
Banker DCF All 9 of 9 cells of the bank's own value grid, across three discount rates and three exit multiples 0.006% $1.2m on $19.2bn — the filing rounds to whole millions.

Where we differ on purpose

Three convention forks are documented, not "fixed": escalation stated as real vs nominal, tax basis reduction, and a statutory rounding step. Each is a modelling choice the user must make, and each is now written down.

Decimal, not floating point

We measured this. Against a float64 reference our decimal answer differs in the thirteenth significant figure — and float64 agrees more closely only because it reproduces the source's rounding errors too.

What we can't yet check

Two axes of the ABS grid — prepayment speeds and note classes — need capabilities we don't have. On the HUD case, the funding-gap solve and the soft-debt repayment likewise. We take what we can and say so plainly.

05
The payoff

Eleven defects our own tests could never have found

This is the argument for the programme. Each of these was live, none was caught by in-house references, and several had shipped in every release.

Weighted average life was 23% wrong on short deals

We measured a bond's life from period zero; the market measures from the closing date to each payment. On a short auto class with a published life of 0.37 years we reported 0.29. Affected every model, in every release.

Prepayments understated by the default rate

We applied the prepayment rate to the balance after defaults; the convention applies it before. Our own reference script made the same misreading, so the two agreed perfectly for months.

Defaulted loans recovered too much

A loan keeps amortising while it sits in foreclosure. We recovered against its original face value — over-recovering ~8% on a 30-year pool and 72% on a short one.

Mid-period discounting did not exist

The convention every project-finance and banker model uses could not be expressed at all. Adding it also uncovered that day-of-month timing was computed as if every period were 30 days long, on every calendar.

0% promotional loans returned "not a number"

Common in auto and retail credit — ~3% of the real ABS pool we tested. Input validation accepted a zero rate, then the maths divided by zero. A wrong answer rather than a refusal.

A tax credit rounded to the wrong tick

The statutory production credit steps in whole dollars per MWh. We first rounded it ten times too finely — which is indistinguishable from not rounding at all, so every unit test passed. Only the external model caught it.

Our published data contract was wrong

Every one of 67 stored outputs violated the schema we publish for consumers — including the field whose only job is to state the format version. Four releases. Now gated.

The pattern worth noticing: in three separate cases our reference script and our engine carried the same mistake, so they agreed. That is the failure mode external validation exists to catch, and it caught it three times.
Also fixed: payment-striking day count · settlement-lag placement · schema drift · CI running five fewer checks than developers ran locally

Open items

The big twoWhy they matter most
Carry-forward stateNo accumulator, no balance a period can add to and a later period draw down. Cash sweeps, revolvers, reserve accounts, loss carryforwards and capitalised interest all need it. Named in the roadmap as the gate on most future domains.
Payment waterfallsAn ordered set of claims paid from a shrinking pot, with seniority and targets. Required by every securitisation and every fund distribution structure.

Curves that vary with age

Prepayment and default rates that change as a loan seasons — the market's standard ramps, and the auto convention. One shared fix serves all three.

Partial first periods

A valuation dated mid-quarter — which is every live deal, since valuation dates are negotiated and fiscal years are not. Currently worked around.

Rounding to a tick

Statutory credits are published rounded to a tenth of a cent. Without it, up to 1.8% out in a single year on a production tax credit.

Judgement call for the team: the two big items are a meaningful build, and they are the prerequisite for three of the six queued validations. Everything else on the list is incremental. The question is whether we take them next, or bank the three unblocked validations first.
