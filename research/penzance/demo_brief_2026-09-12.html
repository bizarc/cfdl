What a Penzance analyst would notice in the cases

The Highlands case is the fuller of the two. It carries the accounts, the seven-tier split and a per-partner return. One Rosslyn is the weaker sibling in four ways that are worth knowing before someone else finds them.

  • One Rosslyn declares partners and a preferred rate but has no waterfall. The case text says the JV tiers are placeholders. In the model there are no tiers at all. Either say so, or add the Highlands split before the meeting.
  • Scenario A's exit, permanent loan and payoff are constants from the generator. "The engine derives the exit" holds for the build-to-core scenario only. Changing growth or the cap rate leaves the merchant-build exit untouched.
  • Scenario A is not asserted. There is no expected-scenarios file, so its 2.02% and 1.094x are reported but not pinned.
  • Occupancy at the Highlands sale. One Rosslyn's text says Evo sold at 60% occupied. The Highlands model's ramp puts Evo near 49% on that date.

None of these blocks the meeting. The honest framing already in both cases, that cost, debt pricing, lease-up pace and the partnership terms are stated placeholders, is the framing to keep. Penzance is the one party in the world that can replace those placeholders, which is the ask.

Run of show, 60 minutes

The spine is their two buildings. Everything before them earns the right to show them, and everything after them is what we want from Penzance. Keep the language section short. Executives buy the Highlands page, not the grammar.

  1. 0:005 min

    Why a language

    The problem they live with: every deal model is a bespoke spreadsheet, assumptions are buried in cells, and no two models of the same building agree. CFDL writes the model as readable text with time, structure and behavior kept apart, compiles it deterministically, and checks it against independent references, period by period.

    Show: cfdl.dev home. The "number and the distribution around it" block.

    Same inputs, same version, byte-identical output. The model is the document, and the document is checkable.

  2. 0:055 min

    The whole idea in ninety seconds

    One stream, one rent, one edit, one re-run. Then flip to the Statement tab so they see a pro forma they recognize: rental revenue, EGI, NOI, DSCR, sale proceeds, monthly to annual.

    Show: Academy chapter 1, change the amount to 4200 * 1.05, run. Then the playground Statement tab with CSV export.

    This is the Argus output you already read. The difference is that the thing producing it is a page of text you can diff.

  3. 0:1010 min

    What it can express

    Three public cases, one point each. Leases: free rent, escalations, expense stops, TI and LC, probability-weighted rollover, forward-NOI exit, every period inside a cent of the reference. Construction: equity first, then the facility, with capitalized interest tying to the dollar. Uncertainty: the renew-versus-relet two-cluster outcome that a blended number hides.

    Show: two-tenant office, One Lincoln Street, then the playground "Stochastic lease rollover" straight to the Monte Carlo tab.

    Skip the Metrics headline on both playground examples. Skip the CRE pack reference page.

  4. 0:2012 min

    Their book: The Highlands, reconstructed

    A completed deal rebuilt from public record only: site plan SP #445, the 2011 land deed, both 2022 tower sales, all 102 Pierce closings from the assessment roll, rents solved back from assessed value at the County's own cap. The facility ties to an independent workbook exactly. The deal returns 11.02% and 2.05x on the recorded exits.

    Then the split. Contributions move each partner's account, a seven-tier waterfall fires once at the last condo closing, and the engine reports each partner's own return. Say "placeholder tiers" out loud before the table.

    Show: /private/highlands. Peak debt, capitalized interest, the partner table, the nine-rate NPV sensitivity and why no NPV is asserted.

    Everything sourced is labeled sourced. Everything assumed is labeled assumed. You are the only people who can turn the second column into the first.

  5. 0:3212 min

    Their book: One Rosslyn, before it exists

    The inverse case: program and land are fact, economics are forecast. Rent derived from Central Place by the County's method. Construction anchored to Highlands' actual spend and escalated by the BLS output index. Two exit strategies over one set of facts: sell in lease-up as they did at Highlands, or stabilize, refinance and hold five years. The engine derives the exit from the twelve months of income after the sale, and scenario A lands within 0.1% of Evo's recorded price per unit.

    The market factor is bracketed by their own history: +32% at Highlands in 2022, −11.6% at Central Place in 2026. Both variants are named scenarios in the run file.

    Show: /private/one-rosslyn. The A-versus-B table, then the market-factor scenarios.

    Land, building, cost and basis are identical in both columns. The holding period is the deal: 1.09x or 1.74x.

    The condo pricing is the weakest input and the case says so. If they push on the 2037 exit per unit, agree: it is the figure most exposed to growth and the factor.

  6. 0:448 min

    How it reaches their desk

    Analysts keep Excel; CFDL is the record behind it. Python SDK with pandas results, an API server for saved runs, VS Code with diagnostics, and the Academy for training a team on it. Position against Argus honestly: lease-by-lease, rollover cycles and statements are at parity in the language; the lease-abstraction UI and importers are not the product.

    Show: surfaces overview without clicking through, and the Harbor Point capstone, a ground-up office-over-retail deal with a construction facility, permanent takeout and a promote, which is the shape of their business.

  7. 0:528 min

    What we want from Penzance

    • Practitioner review of both cases. Both are marked pending exactly that. Their corrections to cost, pace and debt pricing make the cases real.
    • The partnership terms, under NDA, so the split is theirs rather than a placeholder, and the per-partner return means something.
    • A third asset of their choosing, ideally an operating office building, so the rollover and recovery machinery runs on their leases rather than a textbook's.
    • A pilot shape: one analyst, one deal, one quarter, with the Excel-migration and audit services from the commercial plan as the on-ramp.

    You have the record. We have the machine that checks it. Pick the building.
