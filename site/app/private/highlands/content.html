CFDL Benchmark Case · Commercial Real Estate

The Highlands, rebuilt from the public record

A ground-up mixed-use development in Rosslyn, Virginia, modeled in CFDL and reconciled against an independent implementation of the same deal. Every figure below comes from Arlington County records or is marked as an assumption.

Arlington County Site Plan #445 160 monthly periods 2011–2024
The deal

Two towers, one basis

Penzance acquired 1555 Wilson Boulevard on 30 September 2011. The site was redeveloped under a public–private agreement with Arlington County. Seven of the twelve parcels are ground-leased from the County rather than owned.

The project delivered two buildings over one shared podium. The East Building carries both for-sale and rental product, so the costs of the condominium and the apartments are not separable. That is the modeling problem the case sets out to handle.

The record

What is fact, and what is assumed

The case distinguishes the two line by line.

Recorded fact

  • Unit counts and mix by tower, gross floor area, parking and heights, from the 1555 Wilson Boulevard site plan record and its 2018 and 2020 amendments.
  • Land basis of $67,000,000, recorded 30 September 2011.
  • Both tower sale prices, 17 May 2022: $266,455,000 for Aubrey and $334,642,240 for Evo. The County’s Guidebook rounds the second figure to $334,600,000; the model uses the recorded amount.
  • The condominium sellout, unit by unit: 102 of 104 units for $190,186,220 between August 2021 and June 2024.
  • Operating parameters from Arlington's published Commercial Guidebook: a 5.15% loaded capitalization rate for high-rise Metro-adjacent property, 8% vacancy, and $7,511 of operating expense per unit.

Derived, not assumed

Rents are derived rather than assumed. Each tower's assessed value for 1 January 2022, multiplied by the County's own guideline rate, gives the assessor's implied net operating income. The model solves back from that figure to a rent per unit.

Stated assumption

  • Construction cost, debt pricing, and the lease-up pace.
  • The joint-venture split. Those terms are private.
The two towers were sold on the same day, as one transaction. The deed recording dates differ — 18 May for the leasehold, 12 July for the fee — which is why the press reported two separate deals two months apart.
The model

What the language does here

The model is 853 lines of CFDL. It runs on a monthly grid because interest accrues monthly. Four features carry most of the work.

Carrying a balance across periods

A construction loan balance depends on its own previous value. In CFDL that is a field on the entity it describes. prev is the value at the last close. A rule can read only completed values, so a circular reference cannot be written. 

entity asset facility : Asset.Financial {
  // equity funds to its commitment first, then the loan draws
  equity_funded init min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date))
                next min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date))

  balance init 0.0
          next max(0.0, prev + draw + interest - repay)
}

A recorded schedule of closings

Every condominium closing is a declared point. The model reads the real absorption, including the long tail through 2024 that a smooth assumption would hide. 

curve pierce_sellout {
  2021-08: 2056400.00
  2021-09: 8109000.00
  2021-10: 6243000.00
  ... 35 months, every one declared
}

An ordered priority of payments

A distribution waterfall is a first-class construct. Steps pay in declaration order. A step takes what is left if the pot is short, and one step must claim the residual. 

waterfall jv.distribution on entity asset.project {
  schedule on 2024-06 end
  from series_sum("cre.*", 0, time.t)

  pay preferred_inv to party.baupost  = (asset.jv.unreturned - asset.jv.capital) * (1.0 - inputs.sponsor_share)
  pay preferred_sp  to party.penzance = (asset.jv.unreturned - asset.jv.capital) * inputs.sponsor_share
  pay promote       to party.penzance = remaining * 0.20
  pay residual_inv  to party.baupost  = remaining * (1.0 - inputs.sponsor_share)
  pay residual_sp   to party.penzance = remaining
}

The parties are the real ones: Penzance as sponsor, The Baupost Group as equity partner, Mack Real Estate Credit Strategies as construction lender. The tier percentages are not — the partnership's economics appear in no public source, so an 8% preferred return, a 90/10 split and a 20% promote stand in for them. What the example shows is the structure: payments in declared order, each step taking what is left if the pot is short, and a named claimant on the residual.

Where in the period the cash falls

Rent received in January is not the same as rent received in September. CFDL declares where in a period the cash falls, and discounts on that basis. The two conventions differ by 13 basis points of return on this deal while leaving every total identical. A model that reports only lifetime figures cannot distinguish the two conventions.

Five of the six agree exactly. The internal rate of return differs in the seventh decimal place, because the engine rounds the published figure to six.

CFDL evaluates in exact 128-bit decimal rather than binary floating point, so decimal amounts do not accumulate representation error. On a deal of this size the arithmetic contributes no measurable difference.

Two independent implementations of one specification agreeing is evidence that the model is built correctly. It is not evidence that the assumptions are right.
The split

What each partner receives

A development joint venture does not distribute while the deal is live. Cash accrues to the venture and is split once, when the last condominium closes in June 2024. The preferred return and the contributed capital are therefore cumulative balances the model carries forward month by month, not quantities recomputed at the distribution.

Two properties of the model follow from how the pot is defined. CFDL supplies available as the cash an entity produced this period, which on a once-at-end distribution would be a single month rather than the whole deal — so the pot is the streams’ own running sum since inception. And there is no return-of-capital tier: contributions are outflows inside those same streams, so the running sum has already recovered the capital, and what survives to the end is profit.

Limits

What this does not claim

This is a reconstruction from public records. It is not a statement of what the partnership earned.

  • Construction cost and debt pricing are assumptions. They are calibrated so that peak debt sits at 97.5% of the reported facility, but they are not the real numbers.
  • The joint-venture terms are named inputs, not the partnership's terms. Those are private, and nothing here should be read as a view on them.
  • No net present value is reported. The model carries financing, so its NPV would be a levered one, and no source document for this deal states a discount rate.
  • Both towers were sold before they stabilized. The model shows them at 83% and 60% occupancy on the sale date.

Where the model and the real deal differ, the difference is in the assumptions, and the assumptions are all named.

// ------------------------------------------------------------- the facility
// The $380M construction facility (Mack Real Estate Credit Strategies).
//
// Equity funds to its commitment first, the loan draws the residual, interest
// capitalizes into the balance, and sale and condo proceeds repay it. Four
// recurrences, each a fact about the facility, each reading only values that
// are already finished -- `prev` and the cost curves -- which is what keeps
// the whole thing acyclic by construction.
entity asset facility : Asset.Financial {
  equity_funded init min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date))
                next min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date))

  interest init 0.0
           next prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0

  draw init max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - inputs.equity_commitment)
       next min(max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - (min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date)) - min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", edate(time.date, -1))))), max(0.0, inputs.loan_commitment - prev.asset.facility.balance - prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0))

  repay init 0.0
        next min(prev.asset.facility.balance + prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0 + min(max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - (min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date)) - min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", edate(time.date, -1))))), max(0.0, inputs.loan_commitment - prev.asset.facility.balance - prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0)), max(0.0, curve_value("loan_proceeds", time.date)))

  balance init max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - inputs.equity_commitment)
          next max(0.0, prev.asset.facility.balance + min(max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - (min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date)) - min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", edate(time.date, -1))))), max(0.0, inputs.loan_commitment - prev.asset.facility.balance - prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0)) + prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0 - min(prev.asset.facility.balance + prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0 + min(max(0.0, curve_value("dev_cost", time.date) - (min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", time.date)) - min(inputs.equity_commitment, curve_value("dev_cost_cum", edate(time.date, -1))))), max(0.0, inputs.loan_commitment - prev.asset.facility.balance - prev.asset.facility.balance * inputs.loan_rate / 12.0)), max(0.0, curve_value("loan_proceeds", time.date))))
}

// ------------------------------------------------------------ the JV capital
// A development JV does not distribute while the deal is live. Cash accrues to
// the venture and is split once, when the last unit closes, so the preference
// and the capital are CUMULATIVE quantities carried as balances rather than
// re-derived at the distribution -- 17_ordered_waterfall.md section 10.
//
// Both partners fund pro rata and nothing is returned before the split, so the
// two balances only grow. Their difference is the accrued preference.
//
// The preference accrues from CONSTRUCTION START, not from the 2011 land
// purchase: the venture is formed to build, and the land it is capitalized
// with earns nothing for the seven years before there is anything to build.
// Compounding that $67M from 2011 instead consumes the entire promote, which
// is how the assumption was identified.
entity asset jv : Asset.Financial {
  // The facility's equity funding one period back, so a month's contribution
  // can be differenced without reaching two periods behind.
  funded_prev init 0.0
              next prev.asset.facility.equity_funded

  capital init 0.0
          next prev.asset.jv.capital
             + (prev.asset.facility.equity_funded - prev.asset.jv.funded_prev)

  unreturned init 0.0
             next prev.asset.jv.unreturned * (1.0 + if(time.t >= 79, inputs.pref_rate / 12.0, 0.0))
                + (prev.asset.facility.equity_funded - prev.asset.jv.funded_prev)
}

// -------------------------------------------------------------- the JV split
// Penzance / Baupost terms are not public; these tiers are stated assumptions.
//
// `available` is THIS period's netted cash, so on a once-at-end waterfall it
// would draw only the final month. The pot is the streams' own running sum
// since inception instead -- cumulative net cash, after every cost, every draw
// and the facility payoff. The waterfall is named outside the `cre.` prefix so
// the selector cannot match its own steps (E1342).
//
// There is no return-of-capital tier, and that is a property of the pot rather
// than of the deal. Contributions are outflows inside the streams, so a running
// sum of them has ALREADY recovered the capital: what survives to the end is
// profit. Paying capital back out of profit would recover it a second time and
// leave nothing for the promote -- which is exactly what it did.
waterfall jv.distribution on entity asset.project {
  schedule on 2024-06 end
  from series_sum("cre.*", 0, time.t)

  pay preferred_inv to party.baupost  = (asset.jv.unreturned - asset.jv.capital) * (1.0 - inputs.sponsor_share)
  pay preferred_sp  to party.penzance = (asset.jv.unreturned - asset.jv.capital) * inputs.sponsor_share
  pay promote       to party.penzance = remaining * 0.20
  pay residual_inv  to party.baupost  = remaining * (1.0 - inputs.sponsor_share)
  pay residual_sp   to party.penzance = remaining
}
